# Write post

PURPOSE: convert draft or idea into a tight, high-impact blog post
AUDIENCE: software engineers, SDETs, tech practitioners
TASK: transform draft_content into final post; apply options if provided

## Structure

SECTIONS: no required headings; use H2 sparingly only if the post needs clear breaks
OPENING: hook in the first sentence — observation, provocative claim, or brief story beat
CLOSING: one punchy takeaway, question, or call to action; no formal conclusion section

## Style

DENSITY: every sentence earns its place
VOICE: first-person allowed and encouraged; personal experience as evidence
FLOW: conversational but sharp; no academic structure
REPETITION: each idea stated once only
MARKETING: none; authentic and direct

## Content

ANGLE: one clear point per post — lesson, opinion, short story, or tip
SCENARIOS: grounded in real experience — a mistake made, a pattern noticed, a decision taken
CODE: only if a snippet is the entire point; keep it 5–10 lines max
THEORY: skip it; let the story or example carry the insight

## Syntax

ENGLISH_LEVEL: upper-intermediate; natural, readable
SENTENCE_LENGTH: short; punchy preferred over complex
JARGON: minimal; only what the audience uses daily

## Tone

STANCE: honest, direct, personal
PERSPECTIVE: practitioner sharing experience, not instructor lecturing
EMOTION: allowed in moderation — frustration, surprise, conviction add authenticity
HYPE: none

## Output

FORMAT: final post only
REASONING: omit
FRONT_MATTER: H2 title or plain title line; no metadata needed

## Quality checks

HOOK_CHECK: first sentence creates curiosity or stakes
SINGLE_POINT_CHECK: post makes exactly one argument or shares one insight
TRIM_CHECK: remove any sentence that could be cut without losing meaning
